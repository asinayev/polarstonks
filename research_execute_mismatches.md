# Investigation Report: Research vs. Execution Strategy Mismatches

This report details the findings from sampling 10 dates in 2022 to compare the ETF strategy selections generated by the **Research / Backtesting Pipeline** (`research/run_evaluation.py`) against the **Production Execution Pipeline** (`src/am_etfs.py`).

---

## Executive Summary

1. **Perfect Match Under Correct Reconstruction**: When we simulate the production environment by reconstructing the historical data exactly as it would have been seen on each target date $T$ (adjusting prices and volumes for splits/dividends only up to date $T$), the execution signals **match the research signals 100%** (0 mismatches across all 7 evaluated strategies on 10 sampled dates).
2. **Major Mismatches Without Reconstruction**: If the production execution filters are run directly on the raw historical backtesting dataset (`~/data/*.csv.gz`) without reconstructing the "as-of-date-$T$" state, **15 mismatches occur across the 10 sampled dates**. 
3. **Core Root Cause**: The mismatches are driven entirely by **split and dividend adjustments** applied to the historical database after 2022. Because production checks raw `close` and `volume` thresholds while research uses unadjusted `unadjClose` and unadjusted volume formulas, running production code on a future-adjusted dataset filters out eligible assets (due to lower adjusted volumes from reverse splits) or includes ineligible assets (due to higher adjusted prices).
4. **Missing Strategy in Research**: The `arb_etfs` (arbitrage) strategy is implemented and active in production execution (`src/am_etfs.py` lines 115–149) but is **completely omitted** from the research evaluation pipeline (`research/run_evaluation.py`), meaning it has never been backtested in the main evaluation suite.

---

## Methodology

We sampled 10 dates spread across 2022 (representing the third Tuesday of each month):
* `2022-01-18`
* `2022-02-15`
* `2022-03-15`
* `2022-04-19`
* `2022-05-17`
* `2022-06-21`
* `2022-07-19`
* `2022-08-16`
* `2022-09-20`
* `2022-10-18`

For each date $T$, we compared:
1. **Research Signals**: Calculated by processing features on the entire historical dataset (as done in `run_evaluation.py`) and filtering using research criteria on date $T$.
2. **Execution Signals (Reconstructed)**: Calculated by truncating the dataset to $\le T$, reconstructing the adjusted prices/volume as they would have appeared on day $T$ (removing future split adjustments), and running production strategy filters.
3. **Execution Signals (No Reconstruction)**: Calculated by truncating the dataset to $\le T$, but running production strategy filters directly on the future-adjusted prices/volume in the database.

---

## Key Findings & Discrepancies

### 1. Price Threshold Discrepancy (Lookahead Bias in Price Filters)
* **Production Filter**: `close > 7`
* **Research Filter**: `unadjClose > 7`

> [!WARNING]
> **The Problem**: When running on historical databases, the `close` column is adjusted for all splits and dividends up to the download date (2026). If an ETF had a reverse split after 2022, its historical adjusted price is scaled up.
* **Example (YINN on 2022-03-15)**:
  * On this day, `unadjClose` was **$3.11**.
  * The 2026-adjusted `close` in the database is **$62.20** (due to a subsequent 1-for-20 reverse split).
  * **Result**: Research correctly excluded YINN because $3.11 \le 7$. However, the un-reconstructed execution code accepted YINN because $62.20 > 7$, resulting in a mismatch (`YINN` chosen instead of `FXI` for `rally_etfs`).

### 2. Volume Threshold Discrepancy (Split-Adjusted Volume Mismatches)
* **Production Filter**: `volume > 1,000,000` (or `> 500,000` / `> 100,000` depending on the strategy)
* **Research Filter**: `(volume * close / unadjClose) > 1,000,000` (computes unadjusted volume)

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The Problem**: For ETFs that underwent reverse splits, the historical volume in the database is divided by the split factor.
* **Example (ERY on 2022-01-18 and 2022-02-15)**:
  * ERY's actual traded volume on these days was well over 1,000,000 shares.
  * Due to subsequent reverse splits, its adjusted volume in the historical file fell below the 1,000,000 threshold.
  * **Result**: The un-reconstructed execution code filtered ERY out of the `rally_etfs` strategy, whereas research selected it.
* **Example (LABD on multiple dates)**:
  * LABD (highly leveraged bear ETF) frequently reverse splits. Its adjusted volume falls below `500,000` in the database.
  * **Result**: In `deviant_etfs`, the un-reconstructed execution code filtered out LABD and fell back to `SOXS` or `YINN`, creating multiple mismatches.

### 3. Missing Arbitrage Strategy in Research
* The production script `src/am_etfs.py` runs 8 strategies, including `arb_etfs` (which calculates correlation pairs and trading signals based on linear regression deviation).
* The evaluation script `research/run_evaluation.py` only defines and runs 7 strategies: `rally_etfs`, `revert_etfs`, `corr_long_etfs`, `drop_etfs`, `deviant_etfs`, `tru_rally`, and `corr_reverse`.
* **Result**: The arbitrage strategy is completely untested and unvalidated in the backtesting suite.

### 4. Future Leakage in Research Outcomes (But Not Signal Selection)
* In `features.py`, the `rally` function calculates `sell_rally` (the price at the end of the rally) and `sell_rally_date` (the date the rally ends) using `last().over(["symbol_session", "rally_id"])`.
* In `research/run_evaluation.py`, this is processed over the entire dataset (including future dates). Thus, on date $T$, `sell_rally` knows the future date and price when the rally will end.
* While this **does not affect signal selection** on date $T$ (since the selection filters only use the running index `sell_rally_day`), it means the backtest outcomes and exit dates calculated in research are based on lookahead data that would not be available in real-time execution.

## Recommendations

1. **Align Price and Volume Filters**: If the goal is to evaluate strategy performance using historical files directly without custom reconstruction logic, the research scripts should be updated to use unadjusted price and volume columns or vice-versa.
2. **Add Arbitrage Strategy to Research**: Integrate the `arb_etfs` logic into `research/run_evaluation.py` to evaluate its historical performance and ensure it does not introduce unintended risks in production.
3. **Address Future Leakage in Backtest Metrics**: Rewrite the `rally` evaluation outcomes to calculate returns using a realistic exit trigger (e.g. daily checks for the end-of-rally condition) instead of relying on the lookahead `last().over(window)` calculation.

# 2016 Analysis

The same comparison was performed for 10 sampled dates in 2016. The 2016 dataset reveals an **additional, more severe source of discrepancy** that does not exist in 2022.

## Sampled Dates

* `2016-01-19`, `2016-02-16`, `2016-03-15`, `2016-04-19`, `2016-05-17`
* `2016-06-21`, `2016-07-19`, `2016-08-16`, `2016-09-20`, `2016-10-18`

## Key Finding: Polygon Type Reclassification (Universe Lookahead Bias)

In 2016, Polygon's API classified **1,392 tickers as `INDEX` type**, compared to only **2 in 2022**. Of those 2016 `INDEX`-typed tickers, **979 were later reclassified as `ETF`** by the time the historical data files were downloaded (2026). This includes major instruments actively used by the strategies:

| Ticker | Type in 2016 data | Type in 2022 data |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| YINN | `INDEX` | `ETF` |
| ERX | `INDEX` | `ETF` |
| UCO | `INDEX` | `ETF` |
| UNG | `INDEX` | `ETF` |
| NUGT | `INDEX` | `ETF` |
| LABD / LABU | `INDEX` | `ETF` |
| DXD | `INDEX` | `ETF` |
| EWZ | `INDEX` | `ETF` |
| SCO | `INDEX` | `ETF` |
| XLF | `INDEX` | `ETF` |
| TUR, BRZU, TQQQ, SQQQ | `INDEX` | `ETF` |

> [!CAUTION]
> **This is a universe lookahead bias in research.** The research pipeline includes `INDEX` type when loading historical files (needed to capture these instruments in the historical data), but production execution in 2016 would have called `get_stock_list(type='ETF')` and received only tickers Polygon classified as `ETF` *at that time*. Since Polygon classified YINN, ERX, UCO etc. as `INDEX` in 2016, they would have been **absent from the production ETF fetch**, meaning they would **not have been traded** in 2016 despite appearing in the backtest.

### Secondary Pattern: BIB False Inclusion
`BIB` (ProShares Ultra Nasdaq Biotechnology) was classified as `ETF` in 2016, so it correctly enters the execution universe. However, it appears repeatedly as an execution selection that research does not pick. This is likely due to the **split-adjusted price inflation** issue from Finding #1 — BIB's post-2016 reverse splits inflate its historical adjusted `close`, causing it to score highly in ranking even though research (using `unadjClose`) would rank it lower or filter it out.

## Interpretation

The 2016 mismatches represent a **genuine universe construction mismatch** between research and what production would have actually traded:

- The research backtesting report's performance figures for 2016 are based on a **broader instrument universe** (including YINN, ERX, UCO, UNG, NUGT, LABD/LABU, EWZ, XLF, etc.) than would have been available in a live execution run of the same year.
- The actual 2016 live production results (had the system been running) would have excluded these instruments and selected different (or no) ETFs on most sampled dates.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **For years before approximately 2019–2020**, the Polygon type reclassification effect makes the research backtest universe materially different from what production would have traded. Performance figures for early years in the evaluation report should be interpreted with this caveat.

## Additional Recommendation for 2016

**Fix the universe construction**: The research pipeline should be updated to use the `etf_list.csv` file (which lists known ETFs by ticker) as the primary universe filter rather than relying on Polygon's historical `type` column. This would give a stable, consistent universe across all years and match the live execution behavior (which uses `etf_list.csv` metadata via `get_etf_data()`).
